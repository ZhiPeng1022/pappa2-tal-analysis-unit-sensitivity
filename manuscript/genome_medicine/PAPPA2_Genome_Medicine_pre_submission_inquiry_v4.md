# Pre-submission inquiry to Genome Medicine

Date: 8 November 2026

Subject: Pre-submission inquiry: analysis-unit dependence and effect-aware
calibration in human kidney single-cell studies

Dear Editors,

We are considering submitting our manuscript, "Analysis-unit dependence and
effect-aware calibration in human kidney thick ascending limb single-cell
studies", as a Research Article to Genome Medicine.

Single-cell disease studies often describe a cell state as disease-associated
without specifying whether the signal is a between-donor contrast, a tissue
neighborhood association, or a within-donor cell-state correlation. We
developed a unit-aware framework and evaluated it in 106,851 human kidney
thick ascending limb cells from 223 donors. In aTAL2, donor-level and
neighborhood-expression directions were positive while the within-donor
association was negative. A prespecified 400-gene panel showed donor-within
reversal in 19.0% of genes versus 12.7% of expression-matched random sets. A
post hoc effect-aware pairing null gave 20.8%, showing that the initial excess
reflected the selected effect-size distribution rather than broad negative
coupling between units. A smaller neighborhood-versus-within excess remained
threshold sensitive. The same expression-matched versus effect-aware divergence
recurred in two external cohorts. A simulation benchmark distinguished true
opposing effects from concordant and null scenarios.

Two non-KPMP cohorts provided directional support for donor-level PAPPA2
disease elevation, although exact subtype replication and the external
within-donor effect were not obtained. Kidney tubulointerstitial eQTL, PTWAS,
and snATAC analyses are reported as regulatory context and boundary evidence
rather than causal proof. In GSE175759, an independent cohort of 87 primary
kidney-disease samples, the frozen 11-gene TAL-injury score was associated
with lower eGFR after diagnosis adjustment (beta = -8.71 eGFR points per SD;
95% CI -13.79 to -3.63; P = 0.0012). The disease-only estimate remained
negative, whereas the IgA-only subgroup was underpowered. We present this as
one independent clinical-association cohort, not as stable multicohort
validation or clinical utility. The corrected analysis archive is available at
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24042041.

We believe this work may fit Genome Medicine because it addresses a general
measurement problem in genomic medicine and provides a reusable framework for
cell-state studies across diseases. We are not claiming exact external subtype
replication, experimental validation, or a clinical biomarker.

Before submitting, may we ask whether you consider this framework-focused
study, with partial external support but without exact external subtype
replication, suitable for the journal's scope and requirements, and whether it
would be considered for external peer review?

We would be grateful for your guidance.
