**Article type.** We submit our manuscript, "Analysis-unit sensitivity distinguishes donor-level expression enrichment from within-donor coupling in human kidney thick ascending limb states", for consideration as a Research Article.

**The question.** Single-cell kidney studies commonly describe a disease-associated state as a property of a cell type, although donor composition, local neighborhood structure, and within-donor coupling answer different questions. We asked whether these analysis units lead to the same subtype-level conclusion in the human thick ascending limb (TAL).

**What we found.** Using 106,851 KPMP single-nucleus TAL cells, we compared donor pseudobulk, Milo neighborhood abundance, neighborhood-expression association, and within-donor cell-rank association across four TAL subtypes. The units were not interchangeable. In aTAL2, donor-level and neighborhood-expression directions were positive, whereas the within-donor association was negative in combined disease and AKI (FDR = 0.0086 for each). Complete and subset pseudobulk directions agreed in 48 of 60 comparisons, but 12 changed direction. The analysis unit is therefore not a neutral technical choice; it changes which biological quantity is being estimated. A donor-level decomposition showed that the overall PAPPA2 difference was mainly a subtype-expression effect (+0.641) rather than a subtype-composition effect (+0.010). In a separate prespecified 400-gene panel, donor-versus-within reversal occurred in 18.6% of target genes versus 12.7% of expression-matched random sets (empirical P = 0.000999).

**Independent support.** We tested the key directions in two non-KPMP kidney resources. GSE211785 supported donor-level PAPPA2 disease elevation, and GSE254185 showed positive donor-level and neighborhood-abundance directions alongside negative neighborhood-expression and within-donor directions in injured TAL. A donor-stratified signature transfer achieved 93.9% accuracy among assigned held-out cells and 93.9% aTAL2 recall. The external data support a donor-level direction and the general analysis-unit problem, but do not provide exact aTAL2 replication.

**Kidney regulatory context.** In NephQTL2, PAPPA2 was a strong tubulointerstitial eGene (FDR = 1.64e-14) without glomerular eQTL support. We also provide a prespecified boundary analysis: PAPPA2 was not among FDR-significant PTWAS genes, and same-cohort snATAC gene activity did not show higher disease accessibility. We treat this as regulatory context rather than genetic validation and state that boundary explicitly.

**Why Human Genetics.** The manuscript connects human kidney single-cell genomics, donor-aware statistical inference, external cohort transfer, and kidney eQTL context. Its contribution is not a new algorithm or a claim that PAPPA2 causes disease. It is a rigorous demonstration that the unit of inference changes subtype-level conclusions and should be reported explicitly. We believe this fits the journal's readership in human genetics, functional genomics, and disease mechanism.
